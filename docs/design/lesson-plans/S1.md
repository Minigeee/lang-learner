# Stage S1 lesson plans (units 1.1–1.19)

Executive summaries of every S1 lesson, mapped onto the lesson arc ([activity-brainstorm §4](../activity-brainstorm.md)). Units, ideas and prereqs come from [doc 03 §1.8](../../research/03-mandarin-curriculum-and-materials.md); changes to that split are listed under Decomposition notes. Drafted 2026-11-03.

## Stage summary

**Path.** S1 is where lessons really start for this learner (S0 is mostly tested out). It runs: introductions and questions (1.1–1.2) → describing and negating (1.3) → digits 0–10 (1.4), numbers, measure words and possession (1.5–1.7) → asking how and where (1.8–1.9) → time, daily order and modal verbs (1.10–1.13) → shopping, also/all, didn't/don't (1.14–1.16) → yes-no questions, 了 and 在 (1.17–1.18) → checkpoint (1.19).

**At the end the learner can:** follow slow, picture-supported native speech about self, family, time, food and daily life; hear who, what, where, when, how many and how much; tell apart 不/没, 在/有, 二/两, 这儿/那儿/哪儿, 怎么/怎么样, 会/能/可以, 吗/吧/还是, and 了 (new or done) vs 在 (now). Output stays at level 0–1.

**Format introduction schedule.** S0 is usually skipped, so S1 introduces every format itself. The schedule is the default path (table order). The runtime shows a format's one-line intro on its first appearance on the learner's actual path (brainstorm §4.1), so on another valid path the intro can land in an earlier lesson; the "(new; one-line intro)" tags below mark the default path only.

**Stage-wide notes.**
- **Audio.** Practice and examples use AISHELL-3 (most voices) or AISHELL-1. ElevenLabs v4 voices every LLM-generated item and fills thin ideas. Openers use native video spans or AISHELL lines; word cards use the syllable/word sets.
  - **Showdown pairs** that differ only in the target (他是老师 / 他是老师吗) can't come from AISHELL, whose speakers read unrelated prompts. They are either two AISHELL lines that differ in the target but not word for word, or one ElevenLabs v4 voice saying both.
  - **AISHELL transcripts have no punctuation.** Any pool that depends on a line being a question (1.2b, 1.8, 1.14c, 1.17) must be filtered by its question word or particle, not by a question mark. That is why spike B's patterns found 0 lines for 很 + adjective and 什么/谁 (rechecked here: about 80 short AISHELL-3 lines end in 很 + adjective).
- **Clip lessons need a lesson-eligible clip.** The pass-2 read-along needs a transcript, and no S1 clip has one we can fetch. Each Clip lesson's read-along runs on an LLM-written mini-story voiced by ElevenLabs v4, and the YouTube video is the gist opener (`supplemental`). Nothing here waits on a creator's permission. Lazy Chinese says it publishes free transcripts on lazychinese.com, but the site now shows a login; if those transcripts turn out to be fetchable automatically under the site's terms, its clips become lesson-eligible (unchecked; see Concerns).
- **Video length.** A `main` video may run as long as it needs (README Video row). It plays whole inside the lesson's I do. When it carries the explanation, the written I do shrinks to the claim and its figure, and the lesson's minutes count the video's full length. A video that serves more than one lesson plays once, in the first lesson on the path; later lessons point back to it. A video is `supplemental` because of what is known about its contents, never because of its length.
- **Spans in supplemental videos.** Without a transcript or chapters, nobody can locate a line inside a video automatically. Openers that use such a video play its start (the first 60–90 s) or a chaptered speed section, and their gist question comes from the title, description or plot summary. The plan never names a span by what is said in it unless chapters give it.
- **Context ladder.** Practice in S1 climbs word/phrase → sentence. The top rung (a line inside a real clip) is gated on lesson-eligible clips, so no unit reaches it yet.
- **Knowledge-item kinds.** Skill ids use `zh:g:` for grammar and `zh:v:` for a vocabulary theme. `v` isn't one of the kinds the architecture registers (Level 4, Ids); see Review notes. Checkpoints add no skill card, so they have no skill id.
- **Support fading.** Each unit starts with pinyin on and ends with pinyin on peek. 1.19 has pinyin off.

#### 1.1a · 是 links two nouns   (les:shi-links-nouns)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 是 links two nouns (zh:g:shi-links-nouns) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: Six short lines of the form "X 是 Y", heard before any rule. The learner finds that 是 sits between who and what. Practice asks who is what.
- Opener (Discover): 5–6 AISHELL lines (我是学生, 他是老师…); *what's the pattern?* with three options. Mandarin Click 我的朋友 slow-speed chapter (0:08–1:55) as the wider hook.
- Lesson (I do): 是 joins two nouns: who + 是 + what they are. Figure: slot bands *who · 是 · what*. The Yoyo video (1:35, title only) is optional after the core.
- Examples (we do): Lines, 4+ voices, contexts school/family/work; target lit; meaning on tap. Rung: sentence.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, meaning faces. The faces reuse the line's two nouns and swap which one is the subject, so the learner has to parse who + 是 + what rather than match a word. Faces never hinge on 他 vs 她, which sound the same. Climbs phrase → sentence. Fits: identity is a meaning choice.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Discover · C0 (new; one-line intro) · phrase → sentence · school, family · none yet · pinyin on.
- Media: Mandarin Click (supplemental), Yoyo (supplemental); AISHELL-3; word audio for 学生/老师.
- Notes: Pinyin on, slow first. The negation 不是 waits for 1.3b.

#### 1.1b · 叫 gives your name   (les:jiao-gives-name)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 叫 + name (zh:g:jiao-name) · ~5 min
- Teacher's summary: Two intros side by side, one with 叫 + name, one with 是 + job. The learner hears that in an introduction 叫 is followed only by a name, while 是 can be followed by a name or by what someone is. 什么名字 is heard as a chunk; question words come in 1.2b.
- Opener (Showdown): 我叫王明 vs 我是老师, same voice. *Which one tells you a name?*
- Lesson (I do): 叫 is followed by a name: "is called". No ✗/✓ pair: 我是王明 is also correct, so there is no wrong form to show. 是 + name goes in the explanation as one clause, not as an error.
- Examples (we do): Lines, 4+ voices; 你叫什么名字? as a chunk. Rung: sentence.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, sorter face: *gives a name* / *gives a job or role*. Items are 叫 + name and 是 + role only; 是 + name lines are left out, because they'd belong in both bins. The cue is 叫 vs 是 and what follows. Fits: two-way decision on the target word.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Showdown · C0 · phrase → sentence · intros, classroom · 是 (1.1a) · pinyin on.
- Media: none (main `to find`); AISHELL-1/3 (thin, ~10 sentences) plus ElevenLabs v4 for reviews.
- Notes: Skill-card pool is thin; watch the ~20-review check. 姓 is out unless it's in the L1 word list.

#### 1.1c · Meeting someone   (les:meeting-someone)
- Shape: Clip · Skill: greeting and intro chunks (zh:v:greetings) · ~7 min
- Teacher's summary: A real self-introduction first, gist only. Then the learner meets the fixed chunks (你好, 认识你很高兴, 谢谢, 再见) and recalls them in new voices.
- Opener (Story): Lazy Chinese L01 self-introduction, pass 1, no text. One gist question: who does she introduce?
- Lesson (I do): Greetings are fixed chunks; you learn them whole. 3–4 chunks, each on an X7 voice chip.
- Examples (we do): Read-along of an LLM mini-dialogue (two people meeting), ElevenLabs v4, with layers on. Rung: phrase → sentence.
- Practice (you do): C1 Recall: hear a chunk in a new voice, think of its meaning, reveal, grade. Fits: chunks are vocabulary-like.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Story · C1 (new; one-line intro) · phrase → sentence · meeting, phone · 是/叫 lines in the dialogue · pinyin peek.
- Media: Lazy Chinese L01 (supplemental); ElevenLabs v4 dialogue; AISHELL-3 chunk lines; word audio.
- Notes: The read-along can switch to the Lazy Chinese transcript only if lazychinese.com's transcripts prove fetchable (stage-wide notes). The learner probably knows these chunks from dramas, so this is a likely test-out.

#### 1.2a · 吗 makes a yes-no question   (les:ma-yes-no)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 吗 question (zh:g:ma-question) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: The same sentence twice, once with 吗. The learner hears that only 吗 changes, and that the voice need not rise.
- Opener (Showdown): 他是老师 / 他是老师吗, same voice. *Which one asks?*
- Lesson (I do): Add 吗 and a statement becomes a yes-no question. No word moves. ABChinese video (13:22, whole); everything after its 吗 chapter is a preview (是不是 is 1.17's).
- Examples (we do): Pairs, 4+ voices; contexts intros/school. Rung: sentence.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, sorter: *asking* / *telling*. Cue: 吗 at the end. Fits: binary, one-thumb.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Showdown · C0 · phrase → sentence · intros, school · 是/叫 lines reused · pinyin on.
- Media: ABChinese (main, 13:22, whole); AISHELL-3 (320 matches).
- Notes: Links back to 0.12 (a 吗 question needn't rise).

#### 1.2b · The question word sits where the answer goes   (les:qword-in-place)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 什么/谁 in place (zh:g:qword-in-place) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: Five question-and-answer pairs; the learner notices the answer slots into the question word's place. Practice asks what each question is after.
- Opener (Discover): the first ~60 s of Mandarin Click 王小姐's slow-speed chapter, then 5 AISHELL/EL pairs (你叫什么? 我叫…). *What's the pattern?*
- Lesson (I do): The question word stands where the answer will go. No word moves. Figure: question and answer aligned slot by slot.
- Examples (we do): Pairs, 4+ voices; 什么, 谁, 什么名字. Rung: sentence.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, faces: *asks for a thing* / *a person* / *a name*. Cue: the question word. Fits: meaning of the asked-for slot.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Discover · C0 · phrase → sentence · classroom, home · 吗 vs question word (1.2a) · pinyin on.
- Media: Yoyo (main, 2:15, whole), Mandarin Click (supplemental); AISHELL (filtered for questions) + ElevenLabs v4.
- Notes: The spike's 0 AISHELL matches was a pattern artifact (stage-wide notes); the filtered AISHELL pool should pass ≥4 voices. 哪儿 and 几 join the question words in 1.9a and 1.5b.

#### 1.2c · 你呢? asks it back   (les:ni-ne-asks-back)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 你呢? (zh:g:ni-ne) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: Short exchanges where one speaker answers and returns the question with 你呢. The learner learns to expect the other person's answer next.
- Opener (Story): a short native exchange (`to find`), else an AISHELL/EL two-line exchange. *What will she say next?*
- Lesson (I do): 你呢? asks the same question back. Nothing else is needed.
- Examples (we do): Mini-dialogues: the 你呢 line from AISHELL where one fits, the other turns in ElevenLabs v4's 2 voices. Rung: sentence → exchange.
- Practice (you do): C7 What comes next? The exchange pauses after "…，你呢?"; pick the next line from three English options. Cue: 你呢 means the same question returns. Fits: the idea is about what follows.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Story · C7 (new; one-line intro) · sentence · intros, food, work · 吗 (1.2a) · pinyin peek.
- Media: native exchange `to find`; ElevenLabs v4 dialogues; AISHELL-3 (6 lines).
- Notes: Pool depth depends on generated dialogues, and their second speaker is always one of ElevenLabs v4's 2 voices, so the voice check fails here (unit checks).

## 1.3 Describing

Theme: what people and things are like. Adds 很 + adjective, 不, and degree words.

#### 1.3a · Adjectives take 很, not 是   (les:hen-adjective)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: noun + 很 + adjective (zh:g:hen-adj) · ~7 min
- Teacher's summary: Name the misleading strategy first: English says "is tall", so learners expect 是. In Chinese the adjective works like a verb, and unstressed 很 is mostly a link, not "very".
- Opener (Mystery): 她很高, 我很好, 天很冷, with 很 unstressed. *Why is 很 there if nobody stresses it?*

- Examples (we do): Lines, 4+ voices; people, weather, food. Rung: word → sentence.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, sorter: *describes them* (很 + adjective) / *says who they are* (是 + noun). Cue: 很 vs 是. Fits: the parse is the skill.

#### 1.3b · 不 negates the verb or adjective   (les:bu-negates)

#### 1.3c · 非常, 真 and 有点儿 set the strength   (les:degree-words)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: degree adverbs (zh:g:degree-adverbs) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: A scale from 有点儿 (a bit, and unwelcome) through 很 (neutral) to 非常 (strong). 真 is strong too, but it exclaims ("so…!"). The trap is 有点儿: it is mild, yet it signals a complaint.
- Opener (Discover): 有点儿冷 / 很冷 / 非常冷 from three voices. *Which one is the mildest?* The lesson then shows that the mildest one is also the complaint.
- Lesson (I do): The word before the adjective sets how strong it is; 有点儿 is a bit, usually something you'd rather not have (有点儿贵). Go deeper: it can be neutral (有点儿高兴). (Sanity check: the rule was stated as absolute.) Figure: a strength scale.
- Examples (we do): Lines, 4+ voices; prices, weather, people. Rung: phrase → sentence.
- Practice (you do): C2 Odd one out, by strength: four voices and four adjectives; three lines are strong (非常 or 真) and one is 有点儿, or the reverse. Neither the voice nor the adjective can give it away, so the cue is the degree word's strength. Fits: three-alike-one-different is the scale contrast.

- Variety: Discover · C2 (new; one-line intro) · phrase → sentence · shop, weather · 很 (1.3a), 不 (1.3b) · pinyin peek.

- Notes: 太…了 comes in 1.14b; 有点儿 vs 一点儿 in S2. 有点儿 is also heard as 有点 (no erhua) in southern and Taiwan speech; both go in the pool.
