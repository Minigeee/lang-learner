## Stage summary

### Path and outcome

- **Expected path: test-out first.** The learner knows tones and pinyin, so the default is to take the three 0.14 checkpoints (~7 min each, any order; tones first is the best bet), then study only the units they flag (<85%). Passed units get `checkpoint` evidence and skill cards (README synthesis, Completion & test-out).
- **Default path** (no test-out): table order. The tone track (0.1, 0.7–0.10, 0.11a–b) and the segment track (0.2–0.6) are independent until 0.11c (erhua, which needs -n from 0.5a); they merge in 0.12, and 0.13 needs both.
- **Every lesson** opens with *I already know this* (jump to Practice as a test-out) and supports mark-complete.
- **End card:** first-try score and the skill card added. S0 emits no word cards; S1 owns the words. The skill card's review is the tone or sound drill doc 03 asks for.
- **At the end** the learner can: name tones on syllables, pairs and in sentences; tell apart S0's initial and final contrasts; map what they hear to pinyin, including the spellings that hide a vowel; hear sandhi, 一/不, neutral tone and erhua in speech.
- **Input balance** (synthesis conflict 7): each S0 day also gets a CI Clip lesson outside these units. That is where the **Story** opener and **C8 New clip** live; no S0 lesson uses either. Verified candidates for those days: Mandarin Click *张老师和多多* ([EpSnB0JSoDI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EpSnB0JSoDI), 2:47), *买苹果* ([iBxODc9EYOk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iBxODc9EYOk), 3:17), *我爱吃披萨* ([Ebme2W0yF18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ebme2W0yF18), 2:31); Slow & Clear *Immersive Series Lesson 1* ([BDtIRY8RCMg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BDtIRY8RCMg), 6:17). The daily tone-trainer drill (doc 03 §1.8) also runs outside these units.
- **Lesson length:** every core is 5–7 min, so the §4.5 "≤8 min" check passes for all 33.

### Audio and voices (applies to every unit)

Source classes, by rung:

| Rung | Source class | Voices per item pool |
|---|---|---|
| syllable | syllable set: `hugolpz/audio-cmn` (1 native) + local Yoyo pinyin chart (1 native, private bucket only) + ElevenLabs v4 (2) | 4 (2 native); v4 tone items gated (below) |
| word | `audio-cmn` word set (1 native) + ElevenLabs v4 (2) | **3** (1 native) |
| sentence | AISHELL-3 / AISHELL-1, else ElevenLabs v4 | many native |

- The I do and examples prefer native voices; practice never reuses the I do voice (brainstorm §3). At the syllable rung every voice is also heard in the examples, so syllable practice counts as practice, not transfer; novelty there comes from new syllables.
- **The Tones row asks for ≥4 *native* voices.** Only the sentence rung meets that. Unit checks below count native voices for tone units.
- **C2 Odd one out needs four voices per item,** so this plan uses C2 only at the syllable and sentence rungs. A syllable-rung C2 item uses all four syllable voices, so those lessons (0.2b, 0.4a, 0.5b) keep syllable audio out of the I do.
- **v4 tone gate.** v4 isolated syllables are unreliable for tones (tts-grader spike: `DowyQ6` 骂 came out as a 3rd). A v4 syllable or word enters a *tone* item only when the spike's classifier calls its intended tone. The classifier is right on 93–100% of native 1st and 4th tones, 81% of 2nds and 30% of 3rds, and never called a native 4th a 3rd, so most v4 3rds will fail the gate. A voice serves an item set only if it has gated audio for every tone in the set, so voice never predicts the answer. Lessons that hit this say so in their notes.
- **v4 segments are unchecked.** The ElevenLabs check found Whisper inconclusive on single syllables, so no automatic check exists yet for v4 initials, finals or erhua. Segment lessons use v4 with that risk stated (stage concern 1).
- **v4 carriers:** a v4 syllable is voiced from one character with a single reading in CC-CEDICT, never a polyphone (还, 行, 长).
- **Accent filter.** Many southern and Taiwan speakers merge zh/z, sh/s and in/ing. Sentence items for those contrasts (0.4, 0.5a, 0.13b) use only speakers whose dataset metadata gives a northern accent (AISHELL-3's speaker table should carry an accent field; confirm at ingest). Untagged speakers stay out of those items.
- Openers use native audio (syllable set, AISHELL) or a verified video span.

### Support stages (S0 reading of brainstorm §4.3 fading)

Pinyin is often the answer in S0, so fading works on figures, labels and replays instead:
- **full:** figure on; examples labeled with tone-marked pinyin; unlimited ↺ in practice.
- **mid:** figure on tap; labels on; ↺ ×2.
- **lean:** no figure; labels on hold-to-peek; ↺ ×1.

"Labels" here are example labels. A label that is part of a practice item (C3 labels, C0 pinyin buttons) never fades.

### Stage-level concerns

1. **Voice depth (the main build risk).** Only two native voices exist for single syllables and one for words, so no tone unit meets the Tones row's ≥4 native voices below the sentence rung. v4 fills the count, but its isolated-syllable tones are unreliable and only partly checkable (the v4 tone gate under *Audio and voices*): most v4 3rds will fail it, which blocks 0.1c's syllable rung and thins 0.1a and 0.14b. v4 initials, finals and erhua have no automatic check at all. The word rung's 3 voices rule out C2 there. Ways out, all automatic and needing no one's permission (user decision): syllable and word cuts from AISHELL-3 with the spike's MMS forced aligner (connected speech, not citation forms); Wikimedia Commons / Lingua Libre cmn recordings (per-file license check, doc 03); a third v4 voice (adds count, not reliability). Tone Perfect is out: it needs a permission request, and this project contacts no one.
2. **Format check fails by construction** in 1- and 2-lesson units (11 of 14). Only four formats fit perception work. At stage level the rotation holds: no format runs three lessons in a row outside the checkpoints.
3. **Test-out skill cards** may review in a format the learner never met (e.g., C2 after a 0.14 pass). The first review in a new format must show its one-line intro.
4. **C3 with pinyin labels and contour strips** adapts C3 (defined with English glosses). It keeps "the Chinese is always right" and still tests a form-to-label mapping, which in S0 is sound-to-pinyin. It is honest only if: the wrong label is a valid spelling (so reading alone can't find it); it differs from the audio in exactly the lesson's cue; and it never sounds the same as the audio (no 3+3 label on a 2+3 word, no citation 3rd on a sandhi'd syllable). Needs the user's OK.
5. **Label convention:** 一/不 shown with surface tones (as learner materials do), 3rd-tone sandhi in citation tones. The app should state this once. Because a citation label on a sandhi'd syllable doesn't match what is heard, no C3 item outside 0.9 lights a syllable that undergoes 3rd-tone sandhi.
6. **No clip-line rung in S0.** No native clip lines can be timed automatically, so the top rung is AISHELL read speech.

## Unit 0.1 · Tones on one syllable

Theme: the four tones as shapes, then the two confusable pairs. Adds tone identification on single syllables, climbing to the last word of a sentence.

**Unit videos**
- `main` (0.1c) · *Mandarin Blueprint How to Pronounce Chinese 3rd tone* · Mandarin Blueprint · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f3kofJPUQt8 · 2:00 · source: description (the 3rd is low, flat and creaky; full contour only when stressed or alone).
- `supplemental` · *Master Chinese Tones | Pronunciation Training* · Grace Mandarin Chinese · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_Cj3aOSI1w · 12:23 · chapters (1st 2:10, 3rd 3:02, "Solving the 3rd Tone Myth" 4:33, 2nd 7:08, 4th 8:18, neutral 9:29).

- `supplemental` · *Pinyin Tone Listening Test* · Mandarin Click · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TTnH_7wFvdw · 4:17 · chapters (syllables, words, phrases, sentences). An optional self-test, never scored.

**Unit checks**
- Neighbors differ: **pass** (a–b by opener, b–c by format).
- ≥3 formats: **fail** (C0, C2). Adding C3 here would put three new formats in lessons 1–3; the spacing rule wins at the start of the path.
- ≥2 rungs: **pass** (syllable → word → sentence).
- ≥4 voices per contrast: **partial.** The sentence rung has many native voices. The syllable rung has 2 native + 2 v4, short of the Tones row's ≥4 native, and v4 counts only where it clears the tone gate (stage concern 1).
- Contexts: **pass** (isolated syllables, dictionary words, read sentences, teacher video).
- Confusables after both are taught: **pass** (1st/4th inside 0.1c).
- Skill-card pool: **pass** (1,707 syllables; AISHELL sentence-final words).
- Fading: **pass** (full → mid → lean).

#### 0.1a · Each tone is a pitch shape   (les:tone-shapes)
- Shape: Sound · Skill: name the tone of a single syllable (zh:sc:tone-1234) · ~5 min
- Teacher's summary: Start from sound, not the chart. One syllable said four ways, then the four shapes as a figure, then the same shapes in four voices. Practice is plain four-button identification.
- Opener (Mystery): one syllable in four tones from a native voice. "Which one falls?" Syllable set.
- Lesson (I do): idea: *Four tones, four pitch shapes: high and level, rising, low, falling.* Figure: four contours on one pitch scale, the 3rd drawn low with its end rise dashed (0.1c explains why). No ✗/✓.
- Examples (we do): X7 speaker switch on 3–4 syllables, native voices first; X8 A/B on any two tones. Rung: syllable.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, tone buttons. Discriminates all four contours. Rung: syllable. Identification is the target skill and the best-evidenced drill (HVPT).
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Mystery · C0 (new: intro) · syllable · isolated · none · full.
- Media: none. Audio: syllable set, ElevenLabs v4 (tone-gated).
- Notes: on the test-out path, C0's intro appears in 0.14 instead. **TTS flag:** a four-tone set needs gated v4 3rds, which will be rare, so practice may run on the two native voices only (stage concern 1).

#### 0.1b · 1st stays high; 4th falls from the top   (les:tone1-vs-tone4)
- Shape: Sound · Skill: 1st vs 4th (zh:sc:tone1-tone4) · ~5 min
- Teacher's summary: Both start high, so the cue is what happens next. Contrast pairs from one voice, then many. Practice starts blocked, then mixes.
- Opener (Showdown): a 1st/4th minimal pair from one native voice (汤/烫 type). "Which one drops?"
- Lesson (I do): idea: *Both start high. The 1st stays there; the 4th drops fast.* Figure: both contours on one axis.
- Examples (we do): X8 A/B pairs with the voice switch; 4 voices; syllables, then one-syllable words.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, two buttons (1st | 4th). Syllable → word. A two-member contrast, so the direct question is the best fit.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Showdown · C0 · syllable → word · isolated, dictionary words · both members taught here · mid.
- Media: none. Audio: syllable set, word set, v4 (tone-gated).
- Notes: easy for English ears; a likely test-out. **TTS flag:** v4 syllables and words enter only through the tone gate; 1st and 4th are the tones the classifier checks best, so this lesson should keep all four syllable voices.

#### 0.1c · The 3rd tone is low; the dip is optional   (les:tone2-vs-tone3)
- Shape: Sound · Skill: 2nd vs 3rd (zh:sc:tone2-tone3) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: The hardest single-syllable contrast. Charts draw the 3rd as a dip; in speech it is mostly low and often creaky, and the rise comes back only when it is said alone or stressed. The video makes that point. A 2nd rises early from mid; a 3rd sinks first, and any rise comes late. Voices vary most here, so practice uses Odd one out, where voice is never the cue.
- Opener (Mystery): a 3rd said alone (syllable set, with its dip), then the same syllable lit inside an AISHELL sentence, where it stays low. "Same tone. What did the sentence version lose?"
- Lesson (I do): main video (whole, 2:00). Idea: *A 2nd rises early. A 3rd sinks low, often creaky; it rises back only when said alone or stressed, and late even then.* ✗/✓: ✗ wait for the dip → ✓ listen for low.
- Examples (we do): X8 2nd/3rd pairs from the native syllable voices; X7 including creaky voices; sentence-final one-syllable words.
- Practice (you do): C2 Odd one out: four voices, three share a tone, one differs. Sentence-final word (short sentences first); a syllable rung only once v4 syllables clear the tone gate. ~30% 1st/4th sets. Voice quality (creak, pitch range) is the main distractor, and C2 makes tone the only constant.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Mystery · C2 (new: intro) · sentence (syllable if gated) · isolated (examples), read sentences, teacher video · 1st/4th (0.1b) · lean.
- Media: main: Mandarin Blueprint 3rd tone. Audio: syllable set, AISHELL; v4 only if gated.
- Notes: at the sentence rung, the four sentences must share only the final word's tone, and that word must not be followed by a 3rd (no sandhi). Authoring should check both automatically. **TTS flag:** syllable-rung C2 needs all four syllable voices, two of them v4, and the classifier confirms only 30% of native 3rds, so v4 3rds will rarely pass. Until stage concern 1 is settled, practice starts at the sentence rung. The video overstates its case ("NOT a falling-rising"); the idea line keeps the alone-or-stressed exception.

#### 0.2a · Pinyin vowels aren't English vowels   (les:simple-finals)
- Shape: Sound · Skill: map a o e i u to their sounds (zh:sc:simple-finals) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: The trap is reading pinyin with English values. The learner hears several syllables spelled with *e* and names the sound before the rule. Practice maps sound to spelling.
- Opener (Discover): five or six native syllables spelled with *e* (de, ge, he, ke, le). "What does e sound like?" Three options.
- Lesson (I do): idea: *a o e i u have their own sounds; on its own, e is not "eh".* Figure: the five vowels with their English near-matches. ✗/✓: ✗ *e* as in "bed" → ✓ a relaxed "uh".
- Examples (we do): X7 voices; X8 pairs that differ only in the vowel.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, pinyin buttons (valid spellings differing in the vowel). Rung: syllable. S0's job is matching sound to the pinyin under clips.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Discover · C0 (new face: pinyin buttons) · syllable · isolated · none · full.
- Media: supplemental: Everyday Chinese simple finals; Grace "e". Audio: syllable set, v4.
- Notes: *e* inside *ei*, *ie* and *üe* sounds different (in *ie* and *üe* it *is* close to "eh"), which is why the idea says "on its own". That goes in Go deeper, and practice uses bare *e* only. Bare *o* occurs only after b p m f, where it sounds like "wo"; the figure says so. v4 segments are unchecked (stage concern 1).

#### 0.2b · ü is neither u nor i   (les:u-umlaut)
- Shape: Sound · Skill: ü vs u vs i (zh:sc:u-umlaut-u-i) · ~5 min
- Teacher's summary: English has no ü, so ears file it under u or i. The pairs where ü is written with dots (lü/lu, nü/nu) make it audible. Odd one out at the syllable rung.
- Opener (Showdown): 绿 vs 路, one voice. "Which one isn't u?"
- Lesson (I do): idea: *ü sounds like i with rounded lips; it is neither u nor i.*
- Examples (we do): X8 lü/lu, nü/nu, lü/li; 4 voices.
- Practice (you do): C2 Odd one out (three say one vowel, one differs; sets draw on all three of ü, u, i). Rung: syllable. A vowel-quality call where voice must never be the cue.
  The skill card reviews in this same format.
- Variety: Showdown · C2 · syllable · isolated · u, i (0.2a) · mid.
- Media: supplemental: Yoyo #18, #17. Audio: syllable set, v4.
- Notes: ü written *u* (after j q x y) is 0.6a.

#### 0.12b · Questions don't bend the tones   (les:intonation-over-tones)

### What changed and why

**Phonetics (wrong or over-absolute claims)**
- 0.1c: "rarely comes back up" contradicted the citation syllables practice plays, which do dip. Retitled; the idea now says a 3rd sinks low and rises back only when said alone or stressed. The opener now contrasts a citation 3rd with the same syllable in a sentence, instead of a sentence-final 3rd (often a full dip).
