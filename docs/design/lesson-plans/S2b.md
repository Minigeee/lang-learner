# S2b · Stage S2, second half: units 2.16–2.32

Executive summaries for the second half of S2. Format and sources of truth: [README](README.md). What to teach: [doc 03 §1.8](../../research/03-mandarin-curriculum-and-materials.md), S2 rows 2.16–2.32. Arc, openers and formats: [activity-brainstorm §4](../activity-brainstorm.md).

## Stage summary

**Path.** The learner arrives from the 2.15 midpoint with aspect, time and description. This half adds:
- comparison and degree (2.16–2.18);
- clause links and conditions (2.19, 2.27);
- how long and how often (2.20–2.21);
- complements after the verb: direction, 得, potential (2.22–2.25);
- focus and scope: 是…的, question words with 都 (2.26, 2.28);
- 把 and 被 (2.29–2.30);
- a first taste of real-speed speech (2.31);
- the stage checkpoint (2.32).

**At the end, the learner can:**
- follow who is more, less or equal, and by how much;
- hear where an action went, how well it went, and whether it can happen;
- track because, although, if, only-if and besides across two clauses;
- tell who acted on whom in 把 and 被 sentences;
- keep the thread of unscripted speech through fillers and particles.

**Conventions used below.**
- Rungs: word → phrase → sentence → clip line (brainstorm §4.3). A clip line comes only from media with a transcript the pipeline can obtain on its own: a transcribed open corpus (see 2.31). Global Storybooks is dropped as a source (README, Native audio and TTS). Creator transcripts that would need permission, an account or a download of the video are not assumed anywhere in this plan.
- Support stages (pinyin under the always-visible hanzi): `on` → `peek` → `off`. The first lesson of a unit starts `on` (from 2.27, `peek`), and the last ends `off`.
- Audio classes: **AISHELL** (AISHELL-3 first, AISHELL-1 for longer or written-style lines), **ElevenLabs v4** (gaps and all LLM-generated items), **real media** (YouTube openers; never card audio).
- Every lesson ends with the standard end card. Every skill card reviews in its lesson's practice format.

#### 2.16a · 比 puts the yardstick in the middle   (les:bi-comparison)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: A 比 B + adjective (zh:g:bi-comparison) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: Learners already compare in English, so the only new thing is order: the yardstick sits after 比 and the adjective comes last. The trap is 很, which English speakers add out of habit. Teach 更 and 还 as the intensifiers that are allowed.
- Opener (Discover): five or six short lines comparing two people; which one is taller? The pattern question: what comes right after 比?
- Lesson (I do): A 比 B + adjective; 更/还 may intensify. ✗ A 比 B 很 + adj → ✓ A 比 B 更 + adj.
- Examples (we do): people, prices, weather; four or more voices; phrase → sentence; X11 tint on 比.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, gloss faces ("A is …er" / "B is …er"). Only the order around 比 decides. Card reviews in C0.
- Variety: Discover · C0 · phrase → sentence · 3 contexts · none yet · pinyin on.
- Media: Everyday Chinese 0:28–3:19 and 8:25–9:30 (main); AISHELL.
- Notes: Both voices and the adjective should vary, so the answer never rides on which noun is louder.

#### 2.16b · 没有 says "not as … as"   (les:meiyou-not-as)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: A 没有 B (那么) + adj (zh:g:meiyou-comparison) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: The everyday negative comparison is 没有, not 不比. Learners know 没有 as "don't have", so the misleading strategy to name is "没有 means have-not". 那么 is optional and often heard.
- Opener (Showdown): A 比 B 高 vs A 没有 B 高, same voice. Which one is taller?
- Lesson (I do): A 没有 B (那么) + adj = A is less. 不比 exists but argues back; mined, not taught.
- Examples (we do): same contexts; mix 没有 "have-not" sentences from 1.6 so the learner hears both uses.
- Practice (you do): C3 Catch the mismatch. One English line flips "taller than" ↔ "not as tall as". Card reviews in C3.
- Variety: Showdown · C3 · sentence · people, things · 比 (2.16a), 没有 have (1.6) · pinyin peek.
- Media: Everyday Chinese 3:19–4:40 (main); Harbin Mandarin (supplemental); AISHELL.
- Notes: Spike B marked 2.16-b red on CV alone; AISHELL turned it green.

#### 2.16c · The gap comes after the adjective   (les:bi-by-how-much)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 比 + adj + amount (zh:g:bi-amount) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: English puts "three years" before "older"; Chinese puts it after. 一点儿 means a little, 多了 / 得多 a lot, and a number gives the exact gap.
- Opener (Mystery): 他比我大三岁; where did the three years go?
- Lesson (I do): A 比 B + adj + 一点儿 / 多了 / 得多 / exact amount. Figure: a bar pair with the gap labeled. ✗ amount before the adjective → ✓ amount after it.
- Examples (we do): ages, prices, heights; amounts tinted.
- Practice (you do): C2 Odd one out. Four voices, four different sentences; three give a big gap (多了, 得多), one a small gap (一点儿), or the reverse. The amount word is the only cue. Card reviews in C2.
- Variety: Mystery · C2 · phrase → sentence · ages, prices · 比 (2.16a) · pinyin peek.
- Media: no verified video (`to find`); AISHELL.
- Notes: 一点儿 is new here as a word (1.3 taught 有点儿, not 一点儿). It becomes one side of 2.17a's contrast. 得多 is heard as a chunk; its 得 is not 2.23's.

#### 2.16d · 跟…一样 says they match   (les:gen-yiyang-same)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: A 跟 B 一样 (+ adj) (zh:g:gen-yiyang) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: Equality closes the unit, and it lets the practice sort all three relations. 不一样 negates it.
- Opener (Showdown): A 跟 B 一样高 vs A 比 B 高.
- Lesson (I do): A 跟/和 B 一样 (+ adj); 不一样 = different.
- Examples (we do): clothes, weather, food; new voices.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, three-way faces (more / less / same) mixing 比, 没有, 一样. Card reviews in C0.
- Variety: Showdown · C0 · sentence · 3 contexts · 比, 没有 (2.16a–b) · pinyin off.
- Media: Everyday Chinese 4:40–6:26 (main); Sisi Laoshi (main); AISHELL.
- Notes: 跟 comes from 2.8b, where it means "with"; here it means "compared with" (prereq gap noted above). 和 (1.14) works the same way in this frame, so items mix both.

#### 2.17a · 有点儿 complains; 一点儿 compares   (les:youdianr-vs-yidianr)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 有点儿 + adj vs adj + 一点儿 (zh:g:youdianr-vs-yidianr) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: Both are "a bit" in English, which is the misleading strategy. Position decides: before the adjective it is usually a complaint ("a bit too"); after it, it compares or asks for more.
- Opener (Showdown): 有点儿 + adj vs the same adj + 一点儿, at a stall. Only the position changes.
- Lesson (I do): 有点儿 + adj = a bit too (usually unwelcome); adj + 一点儿 = a bit more, compared or requested. Figure: the word on either side of the adjective.
- Examples (we do): shopping, weather, food; both forms in each context.
- Practice (you do): C3 Catch the mismatch. One gloss flips "a bit too expensive" ↔ "a bit more expensive", with the adjective held constant so position is the only cue. Card reviews in C3.
- Variety: Showdown · C3 · phrase → sentence · 3 contexts · 2.16c 一点儿, 1.3 有点儿 · pinyin on.
- Media: Everyday Chinese (supplemental); no verified main (`to find`); AISHELL.
- Notes: The erhua and non-erhua spellings (有点 / 有点儿) both occur in the pool; treat them as one form. "Usually unwelcome" is a tendency, not a rule (有点儿高兴 is heard); keep neutral or positive 有点儿 lines out of scored items.

#### 2.17b · 又…又 stacks two qualities   (les:you-you-both)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 又 A 又 B (zh:g:you-you) · ~5 min
- Teacher's summary: A frame whose meaning a gloss carries. The listening skill is catching both qualities as a pair. Short lesson; Recall fits a vocabulary-like frame.
- Opener (Discover): five native lines of food and people described with 又…又; what are the two halves doing?
- Lesson (I do): 又 A 又 B = both A and B, usually the same direction (both good or both bad).
- Examples (we do): food, people, places; X11 tint on both 又.
- Practice (you do): C1 Recall. Hear it, recall both qualities, reveal, grade. Card reviews in C1 (self-graded by design).
- Variety: Discover · C1 · phrase → sentence · 3 contexts · 一边…一边 (2.4c), the other paired frame · pinyin peek.
- Media: Yoyo Chinese (main); AISHELL.
- Notes: Its 又 shares a sound with 又 "again" (2.21c); the 2.21c we do mixes them. C1 checks the pair of qualities, so recall is graded on "both A and B", not on vocabulary alone. "Same direction" is a strong tendency; keep mixed-direction lines out of items.

#### 2.17c · 越 ties one change to another   (les:yue-yue)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 越 A 越 B and 越来越 (zh:g:yue-yue) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: One frame, two uses. 越 A 越 B links two changes; 越来越 is the case where time is the first change. Merged from two doc 03 ideas (note 1).
- Opener (Mystery): 越来越冷 vs 越吃越胖; one says "as time goes on", one says "the more you eat". Which is which?
- Lesson (I do): 越 A 越 B = the more A, the more B. 越来越 B = more and more B. Figure: two rising lines, one with a clock on its x-axis. ✗ 越来越很冷 → ✓ 越来越冷 (no degree word after 越来越).
- Examples (we do): weather, cities, habits; new voices; 越…越 with two different subjects (你越说, 他越…) as well as one.
- Practice (you do): C0 Pick, faces ("over time" / "because of the first thing"). Card reviews in C0.
- Variety: Mystery · C0 · phrase → sentence · 3 contexts · none · pinyin off.
- Media: Twin Cities part 2 (main, chapters); part 1 (supplemental, title only); AISHELL.

#### 2.28b · Question words can mean "some-"   (les:qw-indefinite)

#### 2.29b · 把 plus a result says what became of it   (les:ba-result)
- Shape: Pattern · Skill: 把 + thing + verb + result/direction (zh:g:ba-result) · ~6 min
- Teacher's summary: The same frame ending on a result: the door is shut, the cup is broken, the food is gone.
