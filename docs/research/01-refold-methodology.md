# 01 · Refold methodology

**Question:** What is Refold, what are its stages, and what does it mean for the app's flow?
**Researched:** 2026-11-02. **Version described:** the current refold.la roadmap, "Phase 0–7" ("Updated Mar 24, 2026" on each roadmap page) [2][5]. Earlier versions are summarized in [§1.1](#11-which-version-this-describes) because much community advice still uses the old terms.

---

## TL;DR

- Refold is an input-first, self-study method. You acquire the language by understanding lots of native content, and studying (vocabulary, grammar, sounds) only "primes" that understanding. Its sources are Krashen's comprehensible-input theory and Matt vs Japan's Mass Immersion Approach [2][3][35][41].
- The **current roadmap (2026) has 8 phases.** Phase 0 is method orientation, then 1 Foundations, 2 Comprehension (reading with audio), 3 Listening, 4 Speaking, 5 Accuracy (writing), 6 Fluency and 7 And Beyond. Skills come in a "cascade": reading first, then listening, speaking and writing [1][2]. (verified)
- **Every sub-phase has explicit move-on criteria** (word counts, comprehension tests, Habit Health). Each also has a recommended time split across four activity types: **Priming / Interactive / Freeflow / Output** [5]–[18]. These four types replace the older "active vs passive immersion" split. (verified)
- **Mandarin counts as "Distant":** about **2,700 hours to functional fluency**, about 1,500 hours before the Speaking phase, and about 540 hours to the "fun threshold" [21][22]. (verified)
- **Mandarin order:** learn pinyin and the sound system (tones included) first, and hold off on characters at the very start. From Phase 1C, learn characters through vocabulary cards, removing the pinyin support step by step at about 200, 500 and 2,000 words [5][7][26]. (verified) Refold's generic pages say almost nothing about tones specifically. That gap needs other sources (see doc 03).
- **SRS:** start with a frequency deck of about 1,000 words at **5–10 new cards a day** (Refold warns against 20–30) with FSRS scheduling. Switch to **sentence mining** of one-unknown-word sentences ("1T") from Phase 2B on. Delete cards you don't need rather than let a backlog grow [24][25]. (verified)
- **Passive listening:** the current Refold line is that background listening has **limited value** and should not be logged as quality freeflow time [31][32]. (verified) The old roadmap was more positive: it recommended 30+ min a day of passive listening and condensed audio [39]. (reported) This is not a core use case for the app. The user's bedtime and lazy-day use is short *active* sessions with the screen on, plus Netflix watching outside the app that gets logged manually (see Implications).
- **Tracking is central.** Refold says tracking correlates with success. It tracks minutes per activity type and **Habit Health / hit rate** (target 80%+) instead of streaks [6][33][34]. (verified) Refold publishes no outcome statistics beyond FSI-based hour estimates adjusted by a community of "70,000+" learners [21].
- **The user's 25% curriculum / 25% SRS / 50% Netflix plan** matches Refold only in Phase 1A–1B. It should shift toward about 20% SRS and 75–80% immersion by Phase 2. Phases 1–2 in 6 months would take about 750 h ≈ **4.1 h/day**; at Refold's 1–2 h/day pacing, about 12–14 months is realistic (see §8).

## Findings

### 1. Core philosophy

**Acquisition vs study.** Refold separates *acquisition* (intuitive knowledge built by understanding messages) from *study* (learning about the language). Its position is that study supports acquisition but can never replace it. It cites Krashen (1982) and Wong & VanPatten (2003), who found traditional grammar drills less effective than meaning-focused activity with brief attention to form [3]. (verified) This is why Refold avoids grammar drills: grammar study is "comprehension-focused, not drills or conjugation tables", a few minutes a day in Phase 1 [5]. Formal grammar comes back only in Phase 5, to fix specific errors in your own writing [19]. (verified)

**Comprehensible input and i+1.** Refold's CI article roots the method in Krashen's input hypothesis: input slightly above your level, where you understand roughly **70–90%** and fill the rest from context or tools [35]. (verified) It doesn't use the term "i+1". Its practical version of i+1 is the **"1T sentence"**: a sentence in which you know everything except one target word (two at most) [25]. (verified) It also cites Long's interaction hypothesis to justify *interactive* immersion, meaning tool-assisted engagement with input [35].

**Interactive vs freeflow (formerly active vs passive).** The current vocabulary is:
- *Priming*: SRS, grammar, sound and script study.
- *Interactive immersion*: pausing, looking up, replaying. It "builds knowledge".
- *Freeflow immersion*: no pausing or lookups, accepting partial understanding. It builds speed and automaticity [31].
- *Output*: speaking and writing [33][34].

(verified) The 2020-era roadmap instead used **active immersion** (focused attention) and **passive immersion** (background listening while doing something else) [39][40]. (reported)

**The silent period / early output debate.** By Refold's own account, the original roadmap advised near-perfect listening before speaking and started output with writing [38]. (verified) The community connects this to Matt vs Japan's earlier stance, which discouraged early speaking [40][41]. (reported) The 2026 roadmap softens the stance in three ways:
- light pronunciation work such as chorusing starts in Phase 3D [16][30];
- daily output (chorusing, reading aloud, monologuing) starts in Phase 4A at about 20% of time [18];
- speaking now comes *before* writing, because writing first causes overthinking [2][38].

(verified) The remaining rationale is perceptual. Producing sounds before you can hear the distinctions can cement native-language substitutes, so ears come first [30]. (verified) Refold does not use the term "silent period" anywhere we checked [3][35]. In effect, though, a Mandarin learner does about 1,500 hours of input before speaking becomes the focus [21].

### 2. The roadmap, phase by phase

Hour figures below are for **Distant** languages (Mandarin) [21][22]. Small inconsistency: the "How long" article gives Phase 1 as "150 / 200" cumulative, but the Phase 2 cumulative of 750 implies 150 [21]. Its tables also label the third column "Neutral" on phase pages and "Other" on the categories page.

| Phase | Hours (Distant) this / cumulative | CEFR target R/L/W/S [2] |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Foundations | 150 / 150 | A2/A1/A1/A1 |
| 2 Comprehension | 600 / 750 | B1/A2/A1/A1 |
| 3 Listening | 750 / 1,500 | B2/B1/A1/A2 |
| 4 Speaking | 300 / 1,800 | B2/B2/A2/B1 |
| 5 Accuracy | 450 / 2,250 | C1/B2/B2/B2 |
| 6 Fluency | 450 / 2,700 | C1/C1/B2/C1 |
| 7 And Beyond | open-ended | C2/C2/C1/C2 |

Pacing: start at about 45 min/day and build toward about 2 h/day. Effectiveness reportedly drops past about 5 h/day [21]. The "fun threshold" (about 20% of the fluency hours, when native content becomes enjoyable) is **about 540 h, or about 9 months at 2 h/day** for Distant languages [21]. (verified)

**Phase 0 – Immersion Learning.** Orientation articles only (method, acquisition vs study, enjoying the process) [1]. No activities and no exit test.

**Phase 1 – Foundations** (goal: not feel totally lost; build routine and tracking) [4]:

| Sub-phase | Activities | Time split (sample day) | Move on when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1A Absolute Basics | SRS first 100 words; sound study; light grammar; script (pinyin only for Chinese); "Noticing Game" on CI/kids' content | 50% priming / 50% immersion; 1 h ideal, 30 min minimum (15–20 Anki, 5–10 grammar, 5–10 sounds, 30 CI) | 100 words; tried every activity; comfortable daily routine [5] |
| 1B Effort Tracking | Same as 1A, plus logging every session | about 50/50; adjust if it drifts to 60/40 | Consistent tracking; **Habit Health ≥80% over 14 days** [6] |
| 1C Begin Comprehension | Interactive Reading with Audio; Multipass; re-experiencing (dubbed familiar shows); hybrid content (subtitled video); **Character study (Chinese/Japanese)** | 30% priming / 70% interactive; 60 min (20 vocab, 20 CI, 20 dubbed show) | Understand many basic sentences without lookups and many simple sentences with tools [7] |
| 1D Get Comfortable | Add optional **freeflow** on content already studied | 25% priming / ≥50% interactive / ≤25% freeflow; grow 1 h toward 1.5 h | Meeting daily goals; **≥1,000 words**. Readiness test on a simple subtitled show: about ¼ of sentences understood without tools, ½ with tools, ¼ too hard [8] |

**Phase 2 – Comprehension** (goal: follow an adult TV show with target-language subtitles and tools). "Reading" includes subtitled TV and YouTube. Reading comes first because you control the pace [9]:

| Sub-phase | Activities | Time split | Move on when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2A Core Words | Keep working through a frequency deck; adult TV with TL subs | 25/50/25; about 90 min (20–25 vocab, 45 interactive, 20–25 freeflow) | **~2,000 words**; about 80% of adult-content sentences understood with subs and tools [10] |
| 2B Develop Comprehension | **Sentence mining** begins; narrow to a few *domains*; optional graded readers | 20/50/30; 90 min (20 SRS, 45 mining, 25 freeflow) | **~3,000 words**; follow adult TV with subs, using tools only occasionally [11] |
| 2C Solidify Reading | Raise freeflow to equal interactive; remove supports one at a time; mine less | 20/40/40; 100–120 min | **~4,000 words**. Test: new show, watch eps 1–2, then on ep 3 follow the plot and understand 90–95% of words from subtitles alone [12] |

**Phase 3 – Listening** (goal: close the *reading-listening gap*; understand native conversation) [13]:

| Sub-phase | Activities | Time split | Move on when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3A Overcome the Gap | Intensive listening (no subs, pause per line, reveal only when stuck); mining while listening; freeflow listening; ear training 10–15 min a few times a week | 20/60/20; 100 min | Gap noticeably narrower; **≥90% of a vlog with no subs or tools** [14] |
| 3B Choosing a Dialect | Pick an accent (e.g. Mainland vs Taiwan Mandarin) and tune to it; revisit sound study [13][28] | n/a | n/a |
| 3C Core Listening Skills | Transcription; listen-looping; freeflow growing; optional extensive reading; less mining | 10/50/40; 100 min | Enjoy and follow most of an adult show **without subtitles** [15] |
| 3D Native Conversation | Crosstalk (each speaks their native language); conversational podcasts/vlogs; first chorusing | 10/40/50 | 30-min crosstalk with ≤3 lookups and <3 repeat requests [16] |

**Phase 4 – Speaking** ("comfort first, then accuracy"; goal: a real conversation at about B1) [17]. **4A Preparing for Output**: chorusing, uncorrected reading aloud, monologuing. Split is 10 priming / 30 interactive / 40 freeflow / **20 output**. Move on when you can record a 5-min monologue and read aloud for 10 min [18]. **4B Speaking Comfort** is volume conversation, where mistakes don't matter yet. **4X Situational Speaking** is optional and can be done at any time [17]. (verified)

**Phase 5 – Accuracy**: 5A writing comfort, 5B writing accuracy (error analysis plus targeted grammar), 5C transfer to speech. Target about B2 [19]. **Phase 6 – Fluency**: 6A output automaticity, 6B vocabulary gaps, 6C high-volume practice. Target about C1 / "functional fluency" [20]. **Phase 7** is open-ended [1]. These later phases are thin on criteria and far from the app's MVP.

### 3. Mandarin-specific guidance

Refold's Mandarin guidance is spread across generic articles. A dedicated "How to learn Chinese" landing page exists, but it is short and marketing-oriented [36]. The community lives in the **Refold – 汉语 (ZH) Discord**, and a community Notion "Mandarin Guide" exists but couldn't be read (JS-rendered) [46].

- **Order (verified):**
  1. Sound system and **pinyin** first. Sound study should be short and bounded: a few 10–20 min sessions, with real learning coming from exposure [5][28].
  2. **No characters in 1A** [5].
  3. Characters through vocabulary cards and "character study" from **1C** [7]. Characters are learned "through use", not through isolated pre-study of a set number [26].
  4. Common components and radicals are optional; they are absorbed through reading [26].
  5. Handwriting isn't addressed for Chinese. The general script advice is read first, write later, typing beats handwriting [27].
- **Character card progression (verified, [26]):**
  - Words 1 to about 150–200: character + pinyin + audio, with the character treated as an image.
  - Up to about 500 words: drop the pinyin, keep the audio, recall the meaning.
  - Up to about 2,000+ words: try to recall the pronunciation before hearing it, but grade on meaning only.
  - After about 2,000 words: grade on pronunciation too.
- **Tones (partly verified):** No Refold roadmap or library page we read treats tones explicitly. The Sound Study, Ear Training and Pronunciation articles are generic, and the only tone mention is that chorusing helps prosody and tone [28][29][30]. The Chinese landing page says the ear tunes in faster from real speech than from drills. It also argues that drilling tone charts and character lists first is learning "backwards" [36]. The minimal-pairs ear training (10–15 min a few times a week) applies [29]. Olle Linge (Hacking Chinese, a credible Mandarin-specific source) agrees that most learners pick up tones by listening a lot in meaningful context. He adds **targeted high-variability perception training with feedback** for tone pairs that stay hard, e.g. 2nd vs 3rd tone [42].
- **Decks and word counts (verified):** Refold's paid **ZH1K** deck has 1,000 hand-picked words in Simplified characters, Mainland, with pinyin, an example sentence, an image, and **AI-generated** word and sentence audio. It costs $24.99 [37]. Generic milestones are 1k / 2k / 3k / 4k words at the ends of 1 / 2A / 2B / 2C, with about 70 / 80 / 85 / 90% text coverage [23]. Refold gives no Chinese-specific *character* counts, and it acknowledges that defining a "word" is hard (which matters for unsegmented Chinese text) [23][26].
- **Simplified vs Traditional:** not addressed beyond ZH1K being Simplified [37].
- **Suggested starter content** includes learner CI channels and popular dramas. The app needs free, legal equivalents; see doc 03 [36].

### 4. SRS in Refold

- **What goes on cards:** pre-made frequency cards with word, example sentence, image and audio (word and sentence) [24][37]. Mined cards use the original sentence plus audio and/or a screenshot from the scene [25]. (verified) Refold doesn't fix a front/back template beyond the character progression in §3.
- **New cards/day:** **5–10** (complete beginners 5–7). Don't do 20–30 a day. A steady average sustained for months beats bursts [24]. (verified) At 7/day the 1,000-word deck takes about 5 months, which roughly matches Phase 1's 150 hours for Distant languages. (inference)
- **Algorithm and reviews:** enable FSRS. Finish reviews daily until the queue is empty so it doesn't pile up. Most people can ignore Hard/Easy [24]. (verified)
- **Deck size and burnout:** the pre-made deck is about 1,000 cards. After that, switch to mined cards because they're more relevant [24]. Don't mine more than you can review. Delete cards that are useless or already known. Mining alone doesn't build speed, so keep freeflow going [25]. (verified) SRS time *shrinks* over the phases: about 20 min/day through Phase 2, then 10 min in 3C–4A [12][15][18].
- **Mandarin note:** Refold's own deck uses TTS-style AI audio [37]. That conflicts with this app's "native audio only" requirement, and the deck is paid, so the app needs its own word list and native audio (doc 03).

### 5. Passive listening and freeflow (brief, since it's no longer a core use case)

- **Current position (verified):**
  - *Freeflow* means TV, YouTube or podcasts with no pausing or lookups (with or without target-language subtitles). It builds speed and automaticity, not new knowledge, and works best on content you've already studied [31][32].
  - *Background* listening has limited value: stay at least half focused, and don't log background noise as quality time [31][32].
  - Freeflow starts in 1D; freeflow *listening* proper starts in 3A [8][32].
- **Old position (reported):** the 2020-era roadmap had a passive-listening habit step: 30+ min/day, relistening to shows already watched, and condensed audio with the silent gaps cut out [39]. Community skeptics reported little gain from months of unattended listening [40].
- **Sleep research (outside Refold):** one lab study found that replaying already-learned words during non-REM sleep improved recall. It did not show new words being learned [43]. (verified, abstract)

### 6. Measuring progress

- **Track minutes per session, tagged** priming / interactive / freeflow / output. Compare the actual split with the phase's recommended split, and review weekly [33][34]. (verified)
- **Habit Health / hit rate:** the share of days you hit your goal, target ≥80%. Refold prefers it to streaks because one missed day barely moves it [6][24][33]. (verified)
- **Milestones** are the move-on checklists in §2: word counts and practical comprehension tests (subtitled show, vlog without subs, crosstalk), plus cumulative hours against phase benchmarks. The Refold App shows cumulative hours against the benchmarks for your current phase [34]. (verified)
- **Stats:** Refold says tracking is "directly correlated with success" based on its coaching clients, but publishes no data [6][33]. Its hour estimates are FSI figures adjusted by community experience ("70,000+ learners"). The article itself treats what counts as an hour as an open question [21]. (verified as Refold's claim; not independently validated)

### 7. Criticisms and limitations

- **Output neglect:** Swain's output hypothesis and Nation's "four strands" argue that meaning-focused output and language-focused learning each deserve real time, roughly equal to input in Nation's model. Refold delays output until about 1,500 h for Distant languages [21][44][45]. (reported, academic summaries) Refold's own revision partly concedes the point [38].
- **Shaky theory:** Krashen's i+1 is criticized as hard to operationalize. Refold itself acknowledges decades of debate [35][45]. (reported)
- **Time demands:** 1.5–2 h/day of high-attention input is hard for working adults, and early AJATT/MIA-style maximalism (all-day passive listening) drew pushback [40][41]. (reported)
- **Passive listening efficacy:** anecdotal reports of little gain without attention [40]. Refold now largely agrees [31][32].
- **Evidence base:** the results are mostly Matt vs Japan's own and community anecdotes; there are no controlled outcome studies [40]. (reported)
- **Mandarin gaps:** there is little tone-specific guidance. Critics of pure absorption note adults sometimes need explicit tone (and tone-sandhi) work [42]. (reported)

### 8. Validating the user's plan (25% curriculum / 25% SRS / 50% Netflix)

**Mapping to Refold's categories.** Curriculum and SRS are both **priming**, so the plan is **50% priming / 50% immersion**. Compare that with Refold's per-sub-phase targets [5]–[12]:

| Sub-phase | Refold priming / interactive / freeflow | User plan | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1A–1B | 50 / 50 (immersion) / – | 50 / 50 | **Matches.** Refold's 1A day is 15–20 min SRS + 10–20 min grammar/sounds ≈ 25/25 + 50% CI [5][6] |
| 1C | 30 / 70 / – | 50 / 50 | Too much priming: non-SRS study has almost disappeared from Refold's 1C day [7] |
| 1D | 25 / ≥50 / ≤25 | 50 / 50 | Too much priming |
| 2A | 25 / 50 / 25 | 50 / 50 | Too much priming |
| 2B–2C | 20 / 50→40 / 30→40 | 50 / 50 | Priming about 2.5× Refold's target |

**Is "Phases 1–2 in about 6 months" realistic for Mandarin? Candidly, no.** Phases 1–2 for a Distant language total **about 750 hours** [21]:
- **750 h in 182 days ≈ 4.1 h/day**, every day: about 1 h curriculum, 1 h SRS and 2 h Netflix. That is double Refold's sustainable target (start at about 45 min/day, build to about 2 h; diminishing returns past about 5 h) [21].
- **At Refold's own pacing** (1 h/day in Phase 1, about 1.5–2 h/day in Phase 2 [5][8][11][12]), Phases 1–2 take **about 12–14 months**. At a flat 2 h/day, six months gives about 365 h: Phase 1 done (150 h) plus roughly a third of Phase 2, i.e. around 2A.
- **The vocabulary check agrees.** Leaving Phase 2 needs about 4,000 words [12]. At Refold's 5–10 new cards/day [24], six months gives about 900–1,800 cards. Even counting words picked up from immersion and mining, 4,000 in six months implies about 20+ new words a day, the rate Refold warns against. Over 12–14 months, 7–10 cards/day fits.
- A more realistic six-month target is **finishing Phase 1 and being into 2A**, which is roughly 2 h/day. That is about where Refold's ~540-hour "fun threshold" (≈ 9 months at 2 h/day) starts to come in view [21].

**Where the split fits and where it should shift:**
- **Phase 1A–1B (roughly the first 1–2 months):** 25/25/50 fits. **But the 50% immersion should not be adult Netflix dramas yet.** Refold's 1A immersion is learner CI videos, kids' content and dubbed shows you already know, used for the Noticing Game [5]. Adult TV with subtitles only becomes the main material in 2A [10]. In practice the immersion half here is mostly in-app CI video, plus Netflix only for very simple or familiar-dubbed content (we haven't checked what Mandarin content Netflix carries; doc 03).
- **Phase 1C–1D (about months 2–5):** shift to roughly **10% curriculum / 20–25% SRS / 65–70% immersion**. Grammar becomes look-up-as-needed. Immersion should be mostly **interactive**: Chinese subtitles, pausing, lookups. Freeflow only becomes worthwhile in 1D (≤25%) [7][8].
- **Phase 2 (after about month 5):** curriculum should fall to **about 0–5%** (grammar reference on demand), **SRS about 20%** (frequency deck, then mined cards) and **immersion about 75–80%**. Netflix with Chinese subtitles is exactly right here. Interactive should exceed freeflow (2:1 in 2A) until the two even out in 2C [10]–[12].
- **SRS should be capped by minutes, not a percentage.** Refold's SRS stays at about 15–25 min/day whatever the total time [5]–[12], because new-card limits bound it. "25% SRS" is fine at 1–1.5 h/day. At 3–4 h/day it would mean 45–60 min of SRS, which either breaks the 5–10 new cards/day guidance or adds nothing.
- **The "50% outside the app" assumption holds from about Phase 2.** Earlier on, much of the input needs learner-level CI that the app (or YouTube) should supply, along with the tracker's suggestions.

## Implications for the app

### Data model / tracking
- **MUST** log every session with `activity_type ∈ {priming, interactive, freeflow, output, background}`, minutes, `source ∈ {in_app, external_manual}`, content id/title and timestamp. Refold uses the first four types [34]. `background` is our addition, for half-attention input, because Refold says not to count it as quality time [31].
- **MUST** show cumulative *quality* hours (excluding `background`, but including manually logged external watching) against the Distant benchmarks (150 / 750 / 1,500 / 1,800 / 2,250 / 2,700) [21]. Show background hours as a separate, smaller stat.
- **MUST** compute **Habit Health** (hit rate of daily goal over a rolling 14 days, target ≥80%) instead of a streak. Never reset progress for a missed day [6][24].
- **MUST** store the user's current phase/sub-phase and each move-on checklist item: word count, self-assessed comprehension tests, Habit Health.
- **SHOULD** compute the actual activity split per week and compare it with the phase's target split (e.g. 1C 30/70, 2B 20/50/30) [7][11].
- **SHOULD** track known-word count (words graduated from SRS plus words marked known) so the 1k/2k/3k/4k gates are automatic [23].

### Stage-driven screens and flows
- **MUST** have a **Today** screen that builds the day from the current sub-phase's sample schedule [5]–[18]. Example for 1A: SRS 15–20 min, sounds/pinyin 5–10 min, grammar primer 5–10 min, Noticing Game on CI video 30 min, each with a timer that logs automatically.
- **MUST** make the daily split **phase-adaptive**, not a fixed 25/25/50. Start at the user's 25/25/50 in 1A–1B, then follow the §8 shifts. Cap SRS by minutes (about 15–25) and new cards (5–10), not by a percentage. Show "your split vs Refold's target" in the weekly review [5]–[12][24].
- **SHOULD** show a time-to-goal projection: remaining hours to the next benchmark ÷ the trailing 14-day average daily hours. This keeps the 6-month expectation honest (§8) [21].
- **MUST** provide a **Phase gate** screen per sub-phase: the checklist, then a guided readiness test (e.g. 1D: rate ¼ / ½ / ¼ of sentences on a subtitled clip; 2C: episode-3 test). Advancement stays the user's choice, with the app suggesting it.
- **MUST, Phase 1:**
  - Pinyin and tone sound module: short, bounded, native-speaker audio.
  - Frequency-word SRS following the character card progression in §3 [26].
  - CI video player with pinyin/character subtitles toggle and tap-to-lookup (interactive reading with audio).
- **SHOULD** add a **Tone ear-trainer**: minimal pairs and tone pairs with *multiple native speakers* and right/wrong feedback, 10–15 min a few times a week [29][42]. Native audio only, per the brief.
- **SHOULD, Phase 2:** a sentence-mining flow. From the player, save the current subtitle line, clip audio and screenshot as a card. Warn when a sentence has more than 2 unknown words (not "1T") [25]. Offer "delete / mark known" from any card.
- **SHOULD, Phase 2C–3:** a "remove supports" toggle ladder: subtitles off, lookups off, no pausing. Intensive-listening mode (auto-pause per line, reveal subtitle on demand), listen-looping, and a transcription exercise [12][14][15].
- **COULD, Phase 3D–4:** chorusing (loop a 3–15 s clip, record, compare), reading aloud and monologue recorder [18]. Crosstalk and conversation stay outside the app; just let the user log them.
- **COULD:** Phase 5–7 screens can wait. Phase 4 is 1,500+ hours away for Mandarin.

### SRS (feeds doc 02)
- **MUST** default to **5–10 new cards/day** (7 for a beginner), with a visible warning above 15 [24].
- **MUST** keep reviews clearable. Refold's burnout advice implies a pile-up after missed days is the main failure mode; doc 02 should design the graceful-recovery behavior.
- **MUST** support card types: word (character/pinyin/audio/image/example sentence) and mined sentence (sentence + clip audio + screenshot), plus delete/suspend/"already known".
- **SHOULD** apply the staged pinyin removal automatically by known-word count (about 200 → 500 → 2,000) [26].
- **SHOULD** budget about 20 min/day for SRS early, shrinking to about 10 min in Phase 3, and warn when reviews eat the immersion budget [12][15].

### Short-session mode (bedtime / lazy days)

The user's low-energy sessions are short, *active* and screen-on: about 15 min of flashcards or a 5–10 min video lesson. Refold doesn't prescribe 15-minute sessions. The templates below scale each sub-phase's recommended split down to 15 minutes (our derivation from [5]–[18]).

- **MUST** offer a **"Quick 15"** button that builds a session from the current sub-phase. It logs each part under its activity type automatically.

  | Phase | Good 15-minute session | Logged as |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1A–1B | 7–8 min SRS (due reviews, then a few new cards) + 7–8 min of a short CI video with the Noticing Game, *or* one 5–10 min pinyin/tone or grammar primer video [5][6] | priming + interactive (a primer counts as priming) |
  | 1C–1D | 5 min SRS + 10 min Interactive Reading with Audio on one short clip, ideally a re-watch (Multipass) [7][8] | priming + interactive |
  | 2A–2B | 5 min SRS + 10 min sentence mining on a single scene with Chinese subtitles, *or* a 10 min freeflow re-watch of a familiar clip [10][11] | priming + interactive or freeflow |
  | 2C | 5 min SRS + 10 min with supports removed (no pausing) on a familiar clip [12] | priming + freeflow |
  | 3A–3D | 3–5 min SRS + 10 min intensive listening or listen-looping on a 1–2 min clip, or a short transcription [14][15][16] | priming + interactive |
  | 4A | 5 min SRS + 5 min chorusing + 5 min reading aloud. Offer a silent alternative (intensive listening) when speaking aloud isn't practical at bedtime [18] | priming + output (+ interactive) |

- **MUST** classify a "video lesson" by its content. A teacher explaining in English is **priming**. A Chinese-language CI video the user is working to understand is **interactive**.
- **MUST** let a flashcards-only session (about 15 min of SRS) count, logged as **priming**. If priming is far above the phase's target share for the week, the next Today plan should lean toward immersion. Example: 1C targets 30% priming [7].
- **SHOULD** handle the gap with Refold's daily minimum. A single 15-minute session is below the 1A minimum of 30 min/day [5], so a Quick-15 day is a reduced day. Whether it counts toward Habit Health is the user's call (Open question 2).
- **SHOULD** pick content for Quick 15 from material already met in longer sessions (Multipass re-watches, mined scenes) so a short session lands at the right difficulty with no setup [31].

### Manual logging of external watching (Netflix etc.)

The user also watches Chinese shows in Netflix, outside the app. Refold counts this as immersion: subtitled TV counts as "reading" [9], and the tracker only needs rough minutes [33].

- **MUST** have a fast **"Log watching"** form: title (with episode, optional), minutes, subtitle mode (none / Chinese / pinyin / English / dual), and how they engaged (paused and looked things up / watched straight through / half attention). The form auto-suggests a category, which the user can override:

  | How they watched | Refold category |
  |---|---|
  | Chinese (or dual) subtitles, pausing, replaying, looking words up | **interactive** (interactive reading with audio) [7][31] |
  | Chinese subtitles, watching straight through without tools | **freeflow** (freeflow reading with audio) [8][31] |
  | No subtitles, no pausing | **freeflow** (freeflow listening) [32] |
  | No subtitles, pausing to re-listen | **interactive** (intensive listening) [14] |
  | Half attention / on in the background | **background** (not counted) [31][32] |
  | English subtitles only | Not classified in the Refold pages we read. We suggest logging it as `background` or flagging it as low-quality, since comprehension then comes from English rather than Chinese (our judgement; Open question 7) |

- **SHOULD** remember recent titles and default the subtitle and engagement choices for one-tap re-logging. A stopwatch option is fine too; Refold recommends one for accuracy, though rough estimates are acceptable [33][34].
- **SHOULD** check the content level against the current sub-phase. For example, in 1C–2A adult dramas are only appropriate with Chinese subtitles and tools [7][10]. Suggest "log as interactive" or "too hard for freeflow" accordingly [31].
- **COULD** add a manual "add mined sentence" entry after an external session (typed sentence + title/time), since the app can't capture clips from Netflix. Clip-based mining stays in the in-app player.

### Daily prompts
- **MUST** have a morning prompt with today's plan from the sub-phase split, how many reviews are due, and new cards.
- **SHOULD** give a weekly review: actual vs target split, Habit Health, hours to the next benchmark, and gate items still open.
- **SHOULD** have a "lazy day" fallback: when the user opens the app late or tired, offer **Quick 15** (above) instead of the full plan. Also prompt: "Watched anything on Netflix today? Log it." The threshold for a "minimum day" is the user's call; see Open questions.

## Open questions

1. **Simplified or Traditional, Mainland or Taiwan?** Refold defers dialect choice to 3B, but the characters on cards have to be decided on day one. User decision.
2. **Does a Quick-15-only day count toward Habit Health?** It is below Refold's 1A daily minimum (30 min). Options: count it as a reduced "minimum day", require the full goal, or let the user set the daily goal per weekday.
3. **Word list and audio source:** ZH1K is paid and uses AI audio, so it is unusable here. Doc 03 must find a free frequency list (e.g. subtitle-frequency or HSK-based) and native recordings.
4. **Tone curriculum:** Refold says almost nothing about tones. Should the app add Hacking-Chinese-style explicit tone-pair training from week 1, or only when the user struggles? We recommend light from week 1 plus on-demand (SHOULD, above), but this needs confirming against doc 03.
5. **Old vs new Refold advice:** much of r/refold and Discord still uses Stage/active/passive vocabulary. Should the app expose the old terms at all? We recommend no, but we could add a glossary.
6. **Paywalled content not verified:** Refold courses, the Starter Course, coaching material and the Refold App's internal phase recommendations sit behind accounts or paywalls. The community Mandarin Notion guide wouldn't render. The original 2020 roadmap pages now redirect, so old-roadmap claims are (reported).
7. **English-subtitled watching:** should it count as quality input? Refold's pages we read only describe target-language subtitles. This needs a user decision, or a check of Refold's paid material.
8. **Daily time budget and the 6-month goal:** is the user's realistic average closer to 1, 2 or 4 h/day? That decides whether "Phases 1–2 in 6 months" stays the goal (it needs about 4 h/day) or becomes "Phase 1 + early 2A in 6 months, Phase 2 done in about 12–14 months" (§8).
